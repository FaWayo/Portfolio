# Personal Portfolio Brand Direction

> **This document is the source of truth for the portfolio.**
> It supersedes `portfolio-build-context-v2.md`.
> Where the two disagree, this document wins. See `decision-log.md` for the
> specific points where v2 was dropped and why.

> **Project**: The Architecture of a Human-Centered Digital Portfolio
> **Brand concept**: Personal notebook x editorial magazine x interactive portfolio

---

## 01. Brand concept

Personal notebook x editorial magazine x interactive portfolio

This website is not meant to feel like a resume placed online.
It should feel like Fatimah's personal space on the internet.
A place where professional work, writing, faith, interests, photographs, questions, ideas and growth can exist together without needing to be neatly separated into "professional" and "personal."

The visual idea is:
A notebook that happens to contain software, products, writing, photographs, faith, questions, and a person still working it out.

The site should feel like someone opened your notebook and found something unexpectedly beautiful and interesting inside.

---

## 02. Brand purpose

The portfolio should help visitors understand three things:

**What I do**
Software engineering, product building, entrepreneurship, writing and the things you're currently exploring.

**Who I am**
Your personality, interests, faith, curiosity, taste and the things that matter to you beyond your job.

**Where I'm going**
The things you're learning, changing, building toward and still figuring out.

The goal isn't to convince someone that you're impressive.
It is to make them curious about you.

---

## 03. Brand personality

**Core characteristics**

- **Thoughtful** — You reflect on things and don't rush to package every experience into a neat lesson.
- **Curious** — You like understanding how things work and following questions somewhere interesting.
- **Grounded** — You talk about what actually happened, what you actually built and what you're actually learning.
- **Feminine** — Warm, elegant and soft without becoming overly pink, delicate or girly.
- **Faith-rooted** — Faith is part of how you understand your life, not a marketing slogan.
- **Quietly confident** — You don't need to constantly announce your ambition. The work and the writing can show it.
- **Unfinished** — You are allowed to change your mind, explore things and become someone slightly different from who you were six months ago.

**Secondary characteristics**

intelligent, warm, observant, independent, creative, playful, sincere, slightly unconventional

---

## 04. Brand voice

The voice should sound like Fatimah talking to a thoughtful person, not Fatimah writing a corporate About page.

**The voice is:**

- **Simple** — Say what you mean.
- **Personal** — Use "I." Don't hide behind abstract language.
- **Specific** — Talk about real things you've done.
- **Reflective** — It's okay to wonder about things.
- **Natural** — The writing doesn't need to sound perfectly polished.
- **Occasionally playful** — Small observations and humour are welcome.

**The voice is NOT:**

corporate, overly polished, motivational, performatively vulnerable, overly quirky, startup-bro, "personal brand" language, full of buzzwords, trying to sound profound

**The golden rule**

Write like a person, not a positioning statement.

Instead of:
> I am passionate about leveraging technology to create innovative solutions that drive meaningful impact.

Write:
> I like building useful things. I also like figuring out why they should exist in the first place.

---

## 05. Voice examples

**Professional**
> I'm a software engineer and product builder. I've worked across banking software, early-stage products and healthcare.

**Personal**
> I've never been particularly interested in being only one thing.

**Reflective**
> I've changed my mind about what I want to build more than once. I suspect I'll keep doing that.

**Faith**
> My faith shapes how I think about where I've been, where I am and where I'm going.

**Light**
> I have quite a few interests. Some are useful. Some are just there because I like them.

The writing should be allowed to sound like a person who is still figuring things out, not someone who has already figured out the perfect answer to life.

---

## 06. Visual direction

The site has two visual layers.

**Layer 1: Editorial** — this is the structure.

dark charcoal, warm ivory, clean typography, generous whitespace, strong grid, sophisticated layouts, photography, restrained colour

This gives the portfolio credibility and clarity.

**Layer 2: Notebook** — this is the personality.

handwritten notes, clouds, arrows, question marks, exclamation marks, circles, scribbles, underlines, tiny observations, photographs slightly rotated, imperfect lines, little visual interruptions

The notebook layer occasionally breaks the perfection of the editorial layer.
That's the point.

---

## 07. Colour palette

**Primary background** — Deep Charcoal `#242222`

Use for the majority of the site. It should feel warm and almost brown rather than cold black.

**Primary text** — Warm Ivory `#F3EFEB`

Use for headings, body copy, navigation, important information. Avoid pure white `#FFFFFF`. The ivory keeps the dark interface softer.

**Secondary text** — Warm Grey `#B5AEAA`

Use for captions, metadata, dates, supporting information, less important navigation.

**Borders** — Charcoal Grey `#46413F`

Use for dividers, subtle cards, outlines, section boundaries.

**Signature accent** — Dusty Rose `#B88D98`

This is the feminine element. Use it for handwritten notes, doodles, links, hover states, selected navigation, little highlights, underlines, arrows, tiny visual details.

**Deep accent** — Muted Rose `#392F32`

Use for subtle accent backgrounds, hover surfaces, small blocks, occasional visual transitions.

**Colour philosophy**

The dusty rose should be discovered, not announced.

Approximate visual balance:
`70% charcoal / 20% ivory / 7% grey+borders / 3% dusty rose`

The website should never feel like a pink website.
The rose is a signature detail.
Someone should gradually realise: *"Oh, that's her colour."*

---

## 08. Typography

The typography has **two** voices. There is no third.

**01. DM Sans — the reading voice**

Use for: the opening paragraph, body text, project descriptions, long-form
writing, anything a visitor actually has to sit and read. It keeps the
professional side clean and contemporary.

**02. Beth Ellen — the hand**

This is the site's main voice, not a marginal annotation. Use it for: the
greeting, room titles, room list, pills and buttons, captions, short statements,
and the handwritten margin notes. It should feel like your handwriting appearing
inside the website.

Beth Ellen is **never** the reading face: a paragraph in a script font is a
struggle, so anything past a sentence or two is DM Sans. It is also never set
below 18px — a script face under 18px looks weak, not intimate.

No display font. Instrument Serif is retired; nothing replaces it. See
`context/decision-log.md` §16.

## 09. Typography hierarchy

| Level | Example | Font | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major title | In progress | Beth Ellen, large, writes on once | Warm ivory |
| Room title | Things I've built | Beth Ellen | Warm ivory |
| Body | I'm a software engineer and product builder, rooted in faith and curious about what I can create with the life I've been given. | DM Sans | Warm ivory, comfortable line height |

| Notebook annotation | still figuring this one out | Beth Ellen, 18px min | Dusty rose |

## 10. Handwritten visual language

This is a core brand element, not decoration.

**Your doodle vocabulary**

- **Clouds** — Ideas, thoughts, dreams, things you're wondering about.
- **Arrows** — Direction, connection, attention.
- **Question marks** — Curiosity, uncertainty, things you're still figuring out.
- **Exclamation marks** — Excitement, surprise, something that matters.
- **Circles** — Highlighting something.
- **Underlines** — Emphasis.
- **Scribbles** — Casual notes and visual texture.
- **Tiny stars** — Occasional moments that feel special.

---

## 11. Doodle rules

- **Imperfect** — Lines should feel hand-drawn. Not mathematically perfect SVG shapes.
- **Restrained** — Don't put a doodle on every section.
- **Functional** — A doodle should usually: point at something, highlight something, add a thought, create movement, reveal personality.
- **Slightly unexpected** — A cloud appearing in a margin is more interesting than a cloud placed exactly where the designer decided a cloud belongs.
- **Mostly dusty rose** — Primary doodle colour `#B88D98`. Occasionally `#B5AEAA`.

---

## 12. The cloud as a signature element

The cloud can become a subtle recurring motif.
Not a logo. Not an icon.
Just something that appears throughout the site.

For example:

*IN PROGRESS* with a little cloud nearby containing: *"things I'm thinking about"*

Or: *WRITINGS* with: *"things I've been thinking about lately"* inside a small cloud.

The cloud represents the things currently taking up space in your head.
It gives the site a visual thread.

---

## 13. Handwritten copy

The handwritten layer should add commentary, not repeat the main content.

| Main copy | Margin note |
|---|---|
| I've worked across banking software, healthcare products and early-stage startups. | *quite the collection.* |
| In Progress | *no title, just notes.* |
| Things I've built | *this is where things get serious ->* |

That little bit of commentary is what makes the site feel like you, rather than a designer's interpretation of you.

---

## 14. Photography

Photography should feel like evidence of an actual life.
Not stock imagery. Not overly staged personal-brand photography.

Use: portraits, candid moments, places, objects, books, clothes, food, workspaces, travel, little details, imperfect photographs.

Some photographs can be polished. Some can be casual. Some can simply exist because you liked the moment.

**Image treatment**

Don't force every photo into the same filter.
Let real colour exist.
The dark palette gives everything enough visual cohesion already.

---

## 15. Layout

**Editorial grid**

The underlying layout should be structured. But the content can occasionally escape it.

Use: asymmetric columns, oversized headings, offset images, overlapping photographs, text positioned at unexpected points, large negative space, occasional vertical text, marginal annotations.

**Principle:** Structured underneath, unexpected on top.

This prevents "unconventional" from becoming "someone forgot to align the divs."

**Slide-deck structure**

Each room is a **full-width block**, and consecutive blocks **alternate
between the dark tone and the paper tone**, so scrolling feels like flipping
through slides. The alternation is part of the structure, not decoration.

**Mobile first.** Everything stacks cleanly on a small screen before anything is
designed for a wide one.

---

## 16. Motion direction

Motion should feel physical and human.

**Think:** writing, revealing, sliding, drifting, unfolding, settling.

**Not:** spinning, glitching, bouncing, exploding, floating 3D objects.

**Signature interactions**

- **Handwriting draws itself** — A line appears as though you are drawing it.
- **Underlines animate** — A dusty-rose underline draws beneath a word.
- **Arrows draw themselves** — Instead of simply appearing, the stroke is revealed.
- **Photographs shift** — A slightly rotated photo settles into place.
- **Notes appear on scroll** — A handwritten comment enters after the main content.
- **Clouds drift** — Very subtly. Not enough to look like weather software.
- **Hover reveals** — Hovering over a project or navigation item might reveal a small handwritten note.

---

## 17. Motion principles

Animations should be:

- Slow enough to notice.
- Fast enough not to become annoying.
- Subtle enough to feel natural.
- Meaningful enough to justify existing.

**One animation only.** As a room comes into view, use a single, very simple
fade. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` at all times.

**Motion should never delay access to the content.**
The site is a portfolio, not an amusement park.

---

## 18. Navigation

**There is no navigation bar.** No top bar, no divided row of links, no fixed
header. The site is one long page and you move through it by scrolling.

Navigation lives in the page instead, in three small pieces:

**1. On the first screen.** Under the opening paragraph, the six rooms appear as
a loose list. Each is a link that scrolls to that room, with its handwritten
note beside it. It should read like part of the welcome, not like a menu.

| Room | Handwritten note |
|---|---|
| ME | *where I come from* |
| WORK | *things I've actually built* |
| WRITINGS | *things I've been writing about ->* |
| LIFE | *the other parts of me* |
| IN PROGRESS | *notes, mostly* |
| LET'S TALK | *no reason needed* |

**2. A floating pill.** After the first screen, a small quiet pill sits at the
bottom centre showing the name of the room you are in. Tapping it opens the six
rooms in a soft overlay. It must never cover photographs in the LIFE room on
mobile.

**3. A signature.** A small `Fatimah` in Beth Ellen at the top left of each
room, scrolling back to the top. A signature, not a bar.

---

## 19. Homepage direction

The homepage should introduce you immediately.

**The site opens directly on this.** There is no separate intro page, no
`[ enter ]` button, no reveal transition, and nothing replays on load. The
first screen *is* the page.

**Main copy**

> Hi, I'm Fatimah.
>
> I'm a software engineer and product builder, rooted in faith and curious about what I can create with the life I've been given.

There is no tagline under the name.

Then a handwritten note somewhere nearby:

`hello ->` or `you're here.`

...with a small cloud.

The first screen should feel warm and wholesome. Generous space, one photo if
there is one, no dividers, no boxes.

The homepage should not contain your entire biography.
It should make someone want to keep exploring.

---

## 20. Section identities

**ME** — Mood: quiet, personal. More typography and portrait. Handwritten notes can reveal small pieces of personality.

**WORK** — Mood: structured, confident. Projects, roles and actual contributions. The visual language becomes slightly more grid-based. Handwritten notes provide commentary.

**WRITINGS** — Mood: reading desk. Medium posts, dates, titles, short descriptions. Occasional clouds and annotations.

**LIFE** — Mood: visual scrapbook. Photography, interests, small fragments, little captions, minimal explanation.

**IN PROGRESS** — Mood: reflective. More whitespace, a large Beth Ellen title, handwritten notes, questions. Things you're learning, things you've changed your mind about, things you're praying about.

**LET'S TALK** — Mood: warm and open.

Not just "Contact me." Instead: *I want to work with you / I want to mentor you / I'd like you to mentor me / I want to exchange ideas / I want to build something together / I just want to say hi.*

And somewhere: *you don't need a very good reason.* — Beth Ellen, dusty rose, tiny arrow. Done.

---

## 21. Logo / name treatment

Don't create a complicated logo.
Your name is enough.

**Primary** — *Fatimah Wayo* in DM Sans.

**Secondary personal mark** — *Fatimah* in Beth Ellen, handwritten and slightly rotated. This is the signature that returns to the top from each room. Use it sparingly.

The site shouldn't look like it belongs to a fashion label.

---

## 22. Brand imagery

Visual references should feel like:

personal journals, editorial magazines, annotated photographs, handwritten letters, gallery walls, notebooks, quiet rooms, books, travel journals, printed photographs, marginalia.

**Not:** SaaS dashboards, tech startup landing pages, neon cyberpunk, generic "female founder" branding, corporate headshots, motivational posters.

---

## 23. What to avoid

**Visually**

neon, gradients everywhere, excessive pink, glassmorphism, giant 3D objects, excessive rounded cards, stock photography, excessive animation, decorative fonts everywhere, fake paper textures, literal notebook backgrounds, doodles covering every available surface.

**In copy**

"passionate about", "impact-driven", "innovative solutions", "leveraging technology", "empowering communities", "dynamic professional", "results-driven", "thought leader", "AI-powered" when it's not necessary, forced inspirational lessons.

The website should never sound like it was generated from the prompt: *"Write a compelling personal brand website for a female software engineer."*

---

## 24. Brand consistency rules

Whenever you're adding something new, ask:

1. **Does it sound like me?** If not, rewrite it.
2. **Does it look like my notebook?** If not, simplify it.
3. **Does it contribute something?** If not, remove it.
4. **Is the feminine detail subtle?** If not, reduce it.
5. **Is there enough space?** If not, remove something.
6. **Is the animation helping?** If not, kill it.
7. **Does it show who I am, not just what I do?** If not, it probably belongs on the CV instead.

## 26. The north star

If we need one sentence to keep the entire project honest:

> Make it feel like Fatimah opened her notebook, added her work, thoughts, photographs, questions and little doodles, and somehow the whole thing became a website.

That is the identity.

Not "personal brand."
Not "developer portfolio."
Not "founder website."

A personal space, with enough craft that people remember they've been there.

# The 6 rooms

**ME** — Who I am, what I care about, how I see my work and life.

**WORK** — What I have actually made, built, led, shipped, and learned from.

**WRITINGS** — My Medium, essays, notes, and things I've put into words.

**LIFE** — The parts of me that don't belong on a CV. Hobbies, interests, places, style, little obsessions, photographs.

**IN PROGRESS** — What I'm learning, questioning, hoping for, and still working out.

**LET'S TALK** — Work, mentorship, collaboration, ideas, friendship.

---

# The homepage

The homepage should be almost ridiculously simple.
Not a dashboard. Not a resume.

Not:
> Software Engineer | Product Builder | Entrepreneur | AI Enthusiast

We have suffered enough.

Something more like:

> Hi, I'm Fatimah.
>
> I'm a software engineer and product builder, rooted in faith and curious about what I can create with the life I've been given.

The page opens on this. There is no `[ enter ]` and no separate screen in
front of it — the first screen is the page, and the rooms follow underneath.

## ME

This is where we go deeper. Not a biography. Something like:

**A little about me**

> I'm a software engineer by training, but I've never been interested in being only one thing.
>
> I like understanding how things work. I like building. I like learning from people who know more than I do. I like writing when I have something worth saying.
>
> My faith is a big part of how I make sense of where I've been, where I am, and where I'm going.
>
> And I'm interested in having a life that is bigger than my work.

That's important.
Because the site shouldn't make you feel like your entire existence is a GitHub repository.

**Layout:** a large portrait bleeding to the edge, text beside it.

**The facts are present but quiet.** Experience, education and skills are shrunk
to a compact block, or sit behind a small `CV` pill linking your résumé:

`https://drive.google.com/file/d/1QZa7OBHapcTPIZW0hVTr-EeA6Kp4PLtO/preview`

No `EXPERIENCE` / `EDUCATION` / `SKILLS` labels above them. ME should not read
like a CV.

## WORK

This is where we get specific.

Instead of "I have experience in software development and product management," we show **Things I've built**:

- **OMAYA CARE** — Postpartum care, hospitals, mothers, health professionals.
- **PCES** — Banking systems, payment processing, APIs, databases, production software.
- **MEST** — Startups, customer discovery, AI integrations, products, experiments.
- **SMS GATEWAY** — FastAPI, async integrations, provider abstraction, testing, production bugs, the glamorous life of backend engineering.

Each one gets its own story.
And crucially: **What I actually did.**
Not just what the company does.
That's where your portfolio becomes much stronger than your CV.

**Layout:** one card per project, same shape each time, with an image placeholder
and a small pill label.

## WRITINGS

This can be visually very quiet.

**Things I've written**

Then your Medium posts.

Above them, a little line:
> Sometimes I understand what I'm thinking only after I've written it down.

That's much more you than "Explore my insights and perspectives."
(I may have to confiscate the internet from whoever keeps writing those phrases.)

## LIFE

This should be one of the most visually interesting sections.

Not "My Hobbies" — because that sounds like a school application.

Instead: **When I'm not working**

**Layout:** a photo grid beside the title, and one short line.

Then photographs, little fragments and categories. **Things I enjoy:**

books, fashion, music, fitness, travel, food, new places, learning random things, good conversations, quiet moments, trying something I've never done before.

And don't explain every one.
A photograph of you somewhere interesting can say more than 150 words about "my passion for exploration."

## IN PROGRESS

This is the emotional centre of the site.
It should feel different from everything else. Maybe almost like a personal journal.

It has **no title.** The room keeps its journal feel and its entry types, but
the phrase is gone — not renamed, just gone. Nothing here says "becoming",
"journey" or "evolving". In the navigation list it is simply `IN PROGRESS`.

> There are things I know I want.
> Things I'm still figuring out.
> Things I haven't even thought of yet.
> I'm learning to be comfortable with all three.

Then little entries:

- **Currently learning**
- **Something I've changed my mind about**
- **Something I want to get better at**
- **Something I'm grateful for**
- **Something I'm praying about**
- **Something I'm excited about**

This section changes as the notes do. That's the point.

## LET'S TALK

This is where the portfolio does something genuinely special.

Instead of one boring "CONTACT ME":

**Why are you here?**

- I want to work with you -> **Work**
- I want to mentor you -> **Mentorship**
- I'd like you to mentor me -> **Learning**
- I want to build something with you -> **Collaboration**
- I think we'd have something interesting to talk about -> **Ideas**
- I just want to say hi -> **Hello**

That last one makes the entire site feel more human.

**Layout:** a large heading, then one small rounded pill per intent.
